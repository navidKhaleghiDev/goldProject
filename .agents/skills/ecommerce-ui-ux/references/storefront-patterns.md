# Storefront patterns

Use only the sections relevant to the requested surface.

## Navigation and discovery

- Make the logo, primary categories, search, account, and cart predictable.
- Use category labels customers recognize, not internal merchandising language.
- For large catalogs, support suggestions, recent searches, typo tolerance, useful filters, clear applied-filter state, and reversible sorting.
- Keep navigation usable by keyboard and touch. Do not require hover for essential destinations.

## Homepage and landing pages

- Communicate the store's category, value, and intended customer immediately.
- Give the hero one meaningful job and a specific destination.
- Follow with credible paths into categories, collections, products, or education.
- Use promotion modules only with real terms and accessible expiry information.

## Collection and search results

- Make products easy to compare through consistent imagery, titles, prices, rating context, and availability.
- Show result count, sort state, applied filters, and a fast way to clear filters.
- Preserve filters and scroll position when returning from a product when feasible.
- Give zero-result pages recovery options, useful categories, or adjacent products.

## Product details

Place decision-critical content near the purchase action:

- accurate title, price, discounts, and unit pricing when applicable;
- useful media with zoom and meaningful alternative text;
- variant names, selection state, unavailable combinations, and size guidance;
- factual inventory or lead-time information;
- shipping estimate, pickup, returns summary, and total-cost cues;
- prominent add-to-cart action with clear feedback;
- category-appropriate details, materials, care, compatibility, ingredients, or specifications;
- reviews with count, distribution, recency, and verified status when genuinely available.

Reflect the selected variant in the price, media, availability, URL, and cart line item.

## Cart

- Make products, variants, quantities, price changes, discounts, and removal editable.
- Show subtotal and disclose when shipping or tax is calculated later.
- Explain promotion errors beside the promotion control.
- Keep checkout dominant while leaving a clear route back to shopping.
- Do not let cross-sells displace contents or obscure totals.

## Checkout

- Allow guest checkout unless an account is genuinely required.
- Request only information needed for fulfillment, payment, compliance, or explicit consent.
- Use appropriate autocomplete and input modes.
- Keep labels visible, validate after meaningful interaction, and preserve valid data after errors.
- Show progress for multiple steps and allow safe backward navigation.
- Present the final order, recurring terms, delivery, taxes, fees, discounts, and total before payment.
- Prevent duplicate submission and provide recoverable payment errors.

## Confirmation and post-purchase

- Confirm success unmistakably with an order identifier and summary.
- Explain next steps, delivery expectations, contact options, and permitted changes or cancellation.
- Do not rely on email as the only confirmation.
- Offer account creation after purchase without blocking the receipt.

## Responsive behavior

- Design from the narrowest supported viewport and expand intentionally.
- Keep primary actions reachable without covering important content.
- Use sheets or drawers only with correct focus management and dismissal.
- Avoid horizontal overflow, tiny hit targets, and layout shifts from late media.
- Test sticky UI against virtual keyboards, browser chrome, notices, and safe areas.
